THE PARADOX OF INCREASING CORTICAL SIZE
Barry Zeeberg
January 2020
The first organisms were unicellular, whose “brains” were little more than mechanical linkages that coupled a sensed food stimulus to movement towards the denser portion of the food gradient.
Sexual drive, particularly among multi-cellular organisms, eventually evolved and ensured increased genetic diversity via genetic recombination and proliferation of species.
It was only a matter of time before evolution re-used the reward mechanism (specifically the brain’s dopaminergic mesolimbic system [1]) that guaranteed sexual drive so that it became more generally employed so as to reinforce other forms of behavior.

The evolution of enhancement of the sizes and capabilities of the multi-cellular organisms came hand-in-hand with increasing brain size, most notably enlarging the cerebral cortex.
It is not surprising that increased cortical size, which leads to increased intelligence and imagination, would lead to an uncoupling of the original mechanical linkage, and bring
motivation into play. These are examples of what is often referred to as “emergent” properties [2].
Preservation of motivation required our brains to trick us, particularly our minds. The tricks needed to be so well disguised as to be strongly accepted as representing reality. The ultimate trick is that now one of our strongest drives is our insistence to rely on the tricks (as exemplified by the human concept of god and religion) and disparage the (apparently less appealing/attractive) underlying reality. This phenomenon is reinforced by the reward system that had originally evolved to ensure sexual drive, and that was then subsumed to ensure other drives. This tendency and its reinforcement represent the utmost example of self-referentiality, ensuring that the tricks have become “memes” locked in to our entire culture.
This is perhaps the crowning glory of evolution, but evolution only works well for immediate short-term benefit. We should now be more concerned with our long-term quality of life.
Now that we have more knowledge, better technology, and better forms of communication, for the first time in history we have a fighting chance to collectively free our minds and ourselves from the enslavement resulting from the tricks that our brains have evolved to play on our minds and on ourselves.
This breakthrough is ours for the taking, but we must do it before the same technology is used to either enslave us more intensely, or destroy us altogether.
