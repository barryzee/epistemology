GUIDELINES FOR PERSONAL SUBJECTIVE RELIGIOUS BELIEF
1. The human brain is limited in its ability to perceive or understand things that it did not evolve to understand. Do not insist on explanations for inaccessible areas. The correct underlying explanations (if any) will not make sense to your brain. Learn to be more tolerant of ambiguity, and to not force an understanding where there is none to be had.
2. Should not contradict factual external reality [example “age of earth = 5000 years”]
3. Should not confuse your subjective belief with external factual reality [avoid semantic tricks that try to get around this ‘this is my reality’, ‘this is reality to me ’, when not referring to external factual reality, use a different word like belief or feeling to avoid diluting the true meaning of reality .]
4. Your religious beliefs should not target or disadvantage other individuals or cultures.
5. Your religious beliefs should not control or dominate other individuals or cultures.
6. Extend the courtesy to any other individual to respect their personal subjective religious belief, as you would like them to respect yours. Since this is not external factual reality, it makes no sense to try to convince another person to have the same personal subjective religious belief that you have.
7. Keep your personal subjective religious belief personal . Do not form large groups of like-minded individuals. There will be a danger of the group morphing from personal subjective religious belief to societal or political control. Large organizations take on a life of
their own that is not related to the original conception. A large organization will have a drive to survive and expand, often without regard for basic honesty, fairness, and regard for human rights, and will tend to deviate from guidelines # 3 and # 4.
8. A hint that guideline #7 is being violated is that you feel a need to go to a particular place on a particular day to make a public display of your devotion. This need is an important hint to you that you should re-evaluate whether you are engaged in a personal subjective religious belief, or trapped in a pre-defined system of societal control. You do not need an organization, a building, a book, or a priest to tell you what your own personal subjective religious beliefs are, or how you should relate to them.
9. If you have a personal subjective religious belief, there is no need to advertise this. This is a relationship between yourself and whatever conception you have of a ‘god(s).’ This relationship is neither enhanced nor strengthened by someone else knowing about it. If you feel the need to advertise it, perhaps that is an indication that your belief is not as strong as you wish, and you are looking for outside validation. Of course, no such validation can exist for something that is your own imaginary personal subjective religious belief. Perhaps you should review guidelines #1 and #3?
10. Help others to free themselves from the trap of a pre-defined system of societal control. Do not teach them the specifics of your own personal subjective religious belief. Rather, help them to freely come to their own belief. To keep it more accessible, focus on guideline #3, not all guidelines all at once. If you are a parent, do not use your privileged position as a nurturing authority figure to trap your own children. Give them the respect of teaching guideline #3.
11. Dangers of religion – learn to identify them and to avoid them:
• Get accustomed to relying on pre-packaged agendas, rather than fresh analysis – can empower the type of rote thinking that enables political power for demagogue or autocrat
• Religious views can condition real-world attitudes: i.e. , ‘people in Gaza are evil and deserve to die.’ [this is leakage of subjective non- rational thought into real world, as if it is an objective reality]
• If many people have similar religious agenda, they may group together to dominate other individuals; a demagogue can ‘enlist’ the sympathy and a large number of votes of a like-minded religious group
