THE PAST AND THE FUTURE ARE DIFFERENT
Of course, the past has already happened and cannot be changed. In contrast, the future has not happened yet, and has not yet been determined. That is why the common, essentially universal view, is that the past and the future are different.
Inevitably, the future will fleetingly become the present, and thence permanently become the past, that cannot be stopped from happening, and then the newly-formed past (i.e., what we just called ‘the future’) will be as inflexible as the older past. If the past and future are different, then that difference is just momentary.
The past and the future are different, because the future has not happened yet, so, unlike the past, it is changeable. Even if the future has not happened yet and it is not completely determined, it is still partially determined (Table 1).

It may sound silly, but if they are (conceptually) different, then they are (really) different. That is, there must be a physical difference between past and future. But what is it that makes them different? How does the past know that it is the past, and the how does the future know that it is the future?
HOW DOES A BIRD KNOW THAT IT IS A BIRD?
How does a bird know that it is a bird and that it can fly? A bird is an object whose genes happened to evolve so as to give it the physical ability to fly and the ingrained drive to use that ability.
3
A bird does not need an external agent to tell it how to fly, or that it should fly. It is self-contained.
HOW DO SUBATOMIC PARTICLES KNOW WHAT THEY ARE?
What about subatomic particles [2], how do they know what they are and what they are supposed to do? Well, they just do what they are supposed to do because that is what they are. It is quite remarkable that there are just a handful of these particle types, and that each of the myriad particles of a certain type, however and wherever it was made, behaves the way that all the other myriad particles of that type behave. The behavior is internal, not dependent on some outside orchestration. And this happens without a process like biological evolution and genes.
HOW DO PAST AND FUTURE KNOW WHAT THEY ARE?
Does time know when it is in the future or when it is in the past? Is there some outside orchestration, or does a “unit” of time know what it is and how it is supposed to behave? Is a unit of time like a subatomic particle in that sense? Does a unit of time inherently know that it is in the past, and therefore cannot be changed, and does it inherently know that it is in the future and that it can be changed?
And can we even talk of a “unit” of time, since it is not known whether time is continuous, or discrete or quantized [3]. Is there a smallest “unit” of time? We can speak of a discrete unit of a subatomic particle, we can speak of one meson.
4
If we want to attribute to time the same type of self-knowledge that we attribute to subatomic particles, then there must be at least two distinct states or conformations of time, corresponding to past and future.
What is a state or conformation? Well, a protein has a certain conformation [4] - [7], and that conformation might change to correspond to active and inactive forms of the protein (Figure 1).
But a protein is a material object made up of real atoms. We know that a material object can fold in three dimensions in distinct ways to hold the information about the current state. But what is the material of time that could hold a self-contained conformation?

What do we mean by “self-contained?” The conformation of proteins is not really self-contained, as the conformation depends on the environment that the protein is immersed in ( i.e. , is it dissolved in water or in a cell membrane?) Maybe subatomic particles would have different behavior if they were immersed in a different universe?
Notwithstanding these complications, it is next to impossible to envision a material substrate S (yet to be discovered) that could hold two distinct conformations of time. If there is such a substrate, then let us designate by R the rate at which the conformation designating “future” is changed into the conformation designating “past.” R might be associated with the determinant of the Newtonian concept of “flow [8]:”
“ Absolute, true and mathematical time, of itself, and from its own nature flows equably without regard to anything external ”
Since R is presumed to be a finite rate, there must be a “holding area” for the portion of time that is undergoing the conformation change. That is, there is a thin sliver of time that is no longer “future” but not yet “past.” For convenience, we can equate this sliver with “present.” So then “present” does have a finite thickness, and is more or less a staging area for the in process changing of “future” into “past.” This concept was suggested pictorially in Figure 7C of a previous section “THE NATURE OF THE PRESENT.”

Summary Topic
Detailed Description
Present time as the “rate- limiting step” in the rate at which time flows.
Present time is a reservoir for holding the amount of time that is in the process of transitioning from future to past. Future is like the low side of the Panama Canal in Figure 2. The present is like the lock that modulates the water levels from low to high in order to make future compatible with becoming past. As with the Panama Canal, it takes a certain amount of time to complete this modulation. Perhaps that amount of time is in fact the determining factor (technically called “the rate-limiting step”) in the rate at which time flows.
Laminar flow of time
At the risk of sounding a little more technical, the flow of time for future and past might be analogous to laminar flow of a fluid, whereas present might be more analogous to turbulent flow. This analogy begs the question of whether there might be a “hydrostatic pressure” driving the flow of time.
Paradoxic that future existed before past.
When we think of ourselves going from moment to moment, we think of ourselves moving from past to future. If today is Monday, after a while we move into Tuesday. So, we have moved from past to future. However, time is actually flowing in the other direction, namely future is becoming past. On Monday, we think of Tuesday as being in the future, but by Wednesday, we think of Tuesday as being in the past. So, future is becoming past. If future is becoming past, in a sense that paradoxically means that future existed before past.
Flow of time is a steady-state process
Except for the position that is designated as “present,” the flow of time is a steady-state process. That is, consider the snapshot of the flow of time depicted in Figure 7C of a previous section “THE NATURE OF THE PRESENT.” This picture never changes, except that the position of “present” is moving along from right to left. If we move alongside the column of time at a pace that keeps us adjacent to present, it appears to us that nothing has changed.
