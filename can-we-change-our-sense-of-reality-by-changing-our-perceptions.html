MANY THINGS THAT SEEM TO REALLY EXIST IN OBJECTIVE REALITY ACTUALLY ARE INTERPRETATIONS OR OVERLAYS CREATED WITHIN THE HUMAN BRAIN
THIS BEGS THE QUESTION: CAN WE CHANGE OUR SENSE OF REALITY BY CHANGING OUR PERCEPTIONS?
Color does not really exist in objective reality, it is just a perception we have resulting from a complicated process starting with the (actual real world) electromagnetic radiation of a certain wavelength, mediated by detection by rods and cones in our retinas, processing in the lateral geniculate nucleus, occipital lobe, and ending in reconstitution in the temporal and parietal lobes.
Similarly, sound does not really exist in objective reality, nor does pain, nor possibly our sense of the passage of time.

This begs the question: can we change our sense of reality by changing our perceptions? That is, can we change the way our brain processes the signal arising from external reality so that we see ‘green’ as ‘red’, or we experience ‘pain’ as ‘euphoria’, or we experience one hour as one second?
In the modern age of genetic engineering ( e.g., CRISPR), perhaps it would not be too hard to change a key gene. However, the general answer is ‘no’. Once a certain response is ‘penciled’ in, a lot of further evolution occurs and is piled on top of the original mutation. The question is whether things are modular enough to allow a simple gene modification, after the fact, to suffice in the face of the huge complexity of pre-existing brain networks.
There is also another reason that things might fail. Even if CRISPR works, it does not mean that there is any evolutionary pathway (in the real world) that would provide this same (alternative) end result. For all we know, the ‘arbitrary’ actual outcome of evolution might be the only outcome that
could have evolved. Perhaps some intermediate evolutionary result was just too ‘unsurvivable’, or perhaps the alternative proteins and mRNAs etc . might interact destructively with other molecular species. Although in principle we could have evolved so that the sensations of green and red are switched, it might in fact be impossible to achieve this through evolution in the real world.
Pain is experienced as pain, because an organism that evolved otherwise would not survive, so we would not see any examples of such. If an animal bites you, and if this were to cause euphoria, you would not ‘learn’ to avoid the animal by avoiding pain.
The same argument regarding colors may not be so definite. For example, if we were to interchange what we see for ‘red’ and ‘green’, perhaps the consequences might not be so severe. In the modern age of genetic engineering ( e.g., CRISPR), perhaps it would not be too hard to interchange the rod cells so that the ‘green’ wavelength receptor and the ‘red’ wavelength receptor switched the pathways they connect to.
