THREE CANONICAL TRAITS
This chapter is, in a sense, a continuation of the previous chapter “ THREE OPTIONS FOR A HUMAN 1: AN INDIVIDUAL’S AWARENESS AND RELATIONSHIP TO UNDERLYING REALITY. ” Three terms in that chapter (“unaware,” “mistaken,” and “aware”) roughly correspond to three terms in this chapter (“Naïve Biological,” “ Culturally Biased,” and “Analytical/Scientific/Intellectual”), respectively.
Each person can be thought of as exhibiting a mixture of three canonical traits. The description of the canonical traits will necessarily be rather abstract. The intention is that each of the three canonical traits will function mathematically like a principal component in a standard principal component analysis (PCA) [1].
First Trait: Naïve Biological
The Naïve Biological trait is “ what your brain, through the process of human evolution, is ‘supposed’ to make you see.” This is what a person would be in the absence of the influences of the other two traits. Nature in the total absence of nurture. A core biological human that is a blank slate in terms of any cultural or intellectual input. A newborn infant immediately taken from its parents, raised by chimpanzees in the jungle.
Second Trait: Culturally Biased
The Culturally Biased trait is “what culture/society/religion condition (a cynic might say ‘trick’ or even ‘brainwash’)

you to see.” This is basically a set of institutionalized beliefs that the individual can get wholesale, with very little effort relative to the amount of content. In contrast to the third trait (see below), this is something that happens to you, in the absence of any effort on your part.
Third Trait: Analytical/Scientific/Intellectual
The Analytical/Scientific/Intellectual trait is “the antithesis of the second trait, it keeps you from swallowing someone else’s story hook, line, and sinker.” In contrast to the second trait (see above) it is something that does not “happen” to you, but rather it requires your own active motivation, analysis, and commitment. This is a life-long attempt to free yourself from the consequences of the second trait.

Data may be acquired by trained professionals designing, administering, and evaluating interviews and standardized objective tests to large numbers of diverse persons. These professionals would include sociologists, psychologists and statisticians. Examples of this type of procedure can be found throughout the classical text by Gordon Allport [2].

HOW TO FIGURE OUT AXES COORDINATES
It may be a little confusing to figure out the relationship between the axes and the location of a point.
Let us take “Person 2” in Figures 1 and 2. Naïve Biological has a value of 60 (Figure 1). Starting at 60 on the Naïve Biological axis (Figure 2), we draw an arrow that is parallel to the Culturally Biased axis (Figure 4). We use the parallel to the Culturally Biased axis ( not the Analytical axis) because the Culturally Biased goes through the value of 0 on the Naïve Biological axis.
We do the same for the other 2 values (30 and 10, Figure 1). There is one location where 3 of the arrows intersect (Figure 4), and that is the correct location for Person 2.
The same logic can be used in reverse decompose a point on the graph into its 3 components.
