TUTORIAL ON GENES, MUTATIONS, AND EVOLUTION
There is an excellent and highly readable on-line tutorial [1] presented by the University of California at Berkeley.
I would urge you to review the entire tutorial. The sections that are most relevant for understanding the concepts in my manuscript are:
•
An introduction to evolution
•
Mechanisms: the processes of evolution
Here is a passage taken from the mechanisms section [emphasis mine], that is essential for understanding the concepts in my manuscript:
Of course, fitness is a relative thing. A genotype's
fitness depends on the environment in which the
organism lives. The fittest genotype during an ice age, for example, is probably not the fittest genotype once the ice age is over.
Fitness is a handy concept because it lumps everything that matters to natural selection (survival, mate-finding, reproduction) into one idea. The fittest individual is not
necessarily the strongest, fastest, or biggest. A
genotype's fitness includes its ability to survive, find a
mate, produce offspring — and ultimately leave its
genes in the next generation.
