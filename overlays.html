OVERLAYS
Overlays
Perhaps one of the most enduring of all human characteristics is to overlay core external reality with human-centered value judgements and inventions. This pursuit is primarily done at a subconscious level. It is so prevalent and so integral to our existence that it is not immediately apparent that it is happening.
Recognizing Overlays
Recognizing overlays is fairly simple to do, once an individual is motivated (or at least not totally defended against) exploring such matters. Imagine that humans have never existed. Then the world would be devoid of overlays. For instance, in a world without humans, kittens would still exist. But kittens would neither be cute nor would they be uncute – in the absence of a human to overlay cuteness, the kitten just “is.”
Why do we see a kitten as cute? Well, it is a helpless baby, and it reminds us of a human baby. A human baby is helpless, and if we did not view it as cute, we might not be as motivated to nurture it. If humans stopped nurturing babies, humans might cease to exist. In that case, we might very well not be around today to discuss the issues at hand.
Religion as an Overlay
Some examples of overlays are easier for us to accept than others. We may merely regret giving up the enjoyment of the perceived cuteness of a kitten, but there are certainly other overlays that we might find much harder to give up. One such example might be the phenomenon of religious belief. Whether god actually exists or not, we do not have direct knowledge of god, so religions are examples of human overlays.
In addition to the methods for recognizing overlays, discussed above, there is an additional technique that is extremely useful. That is, when trying to decide if something is part of core reality or if it is an overlay, you can consider if you might have a very strong vested interest in being able to consider it as part of core reality. How hard would it be for you to let go of something, to give it up? Perhaps the harder it is - the more you resist - the more you should examine if it is an overlay that your subconscious is just not willing to give up. For instance, the more important your religion is to you, then the stronger your vested interest and the stronger a clue that is an overlay.
Career as an Overlay
Many people identify with their careers, and a lot of our self- worth can be tied in with our view of how important or valuable to society we see our jobs. By the time you retire, you have spent decades of your life doing your job, and it would be a blow to find out, after the fact, that everything you did was either worthless, or perhaps even harmful. So, we have a strong vested interest in viewing our career in a certain positive light. Does the strength of this vested interest perhaps prevent us from a more “objective” evaluation of our own careers?
