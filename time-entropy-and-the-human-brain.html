TIME, ENTROPY, AND THE HUMAN BRAIN
One of the mysterious things about time is that it appears to us to flow forward from the past into the future, but not the other way around. There are any number of presentations explaining this in terms of the ‘law’ of increasing entropy, but I noticed something about these presentations that does not quite make sense.
For the sake of concreteness, I am taking one such presentation 1 , but I do not mean to suggest that this one is any better or worse than the others. The following two excerpts suggest the crux of the standard argument:
7:07 ‘With the egg, there is one configuration that is an unbroken egg. However, there are lots and lots of ways an egg can splat.’
7:32 ‘The arrow of time tends towards increasing messiness.’

An important omission is the role that the human brain plays in the interpretation of what is a 'mess/splat' and what isn’t a ’mess/splat.’ This subjective interpretation underlies the example showing that entropy increases. When you properly take the biases of the human brain into account, entropy is no longer coupled to a directionality of time.
The best way to understand these biases is to consider a hand of bridge, because there are definite numerical probability values that take the place of hand-waving arguments.
The number of possible bridge hands 2 is 635013559600, so the probability of any particular hand is 1/635013559600 = 1.57477 x 10 -12 . Each particular hand has this same probability.
Getting all 13 spades has the same probability ( i.e. 1.57477 x 10 -12 ) as any particular 'featureless' ( i.e. , more balanced) hand. It is only the bias of the human brain that makes 13 spades seem special.
Why is it that the human brain is tricked in this way? Because there a certain type of ambiguity in play here. The human brain has a tendency toward categorization 3 . The brain will categorize the hand in terms of the suit distribution, rather than view the hand in terms of its underlying probability.
The probabilities of suit distributions are given in Table 3 of an essential reference 4 .
As shown in my Table 1, the values in this suit distribution table is the key to disambiguation of hand versus distribution probabilities. For the hand with all 13 spades, the probability for the hand is of a similar magnitude as that for the corresponding distribution. But for the featureless hand, the categorization that is performed behind the scenes by the human brain results in an ‘operational’ probability that is misleading by around 11 orders of magnitude.

What does this have to do with the misleading example of eggs 1 and consequently with entropy? The underlying ‘microscopic’ probability of the intact egg is the same as for any broken egg, but our brain misleads us by incorrectly focusing on the categorical macroscopic patterns.
