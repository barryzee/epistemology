DISTINCTION BETWEEN INVENTION AND VALUE JUDGEMENT
There are two kinds of overlays that I mention from time to time, namely
• invention
• value judgement
While these do have something in common, it is essential to differentiate these two concepts.
The commonality is that neither would exist without the existence of the human brain. They are both creations of the human brain – they are both overlays .
An invention is a story that the human brain creates. It may be that the invention corresponds closely to some aspect of external reality, or it may bear no relationship whatsoever to external reality. If sufficiently powerful technologies existed, an invention could theoretically be empirically tested to see if it is true or not.
• god exits
• there is only one god
• there are no gods
• god is merciful
• one race is superior in some way to some other race
A value judgement is what we commonly think of as a value judgement in everyday terms. Unlike an invention, a value judgement does not have a value of true or false, and cannot be tested empirically.
• It is good for a child to get high grades in school
• It is better to marry someone with a good personality than someone who is merely attractive
• It is bad to not believe in god
• It is morally bad to kill someone else
These can often be re-formulated as (non-judgmental) assertions that might have a value of true or false.
• In contemporary American culture, a child who gets high grades is more likely to have a materially satisfying adult existence
• In the Medieval period, a person’s non-belief in the prevailing view of god could lead to punishment by the prevailing church
• Marrying someone with a personality disorder could lead to a stressful existence
• Your existence would be more secure if you live in a society where most people agree that is bad to kill someone else
