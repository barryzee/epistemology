SPECIAL RELATIVITY
Constant Speed of Light
When you see a typical YouTube explanation of special relativity, there is invariably a cartoon character with a silly name on a moving ( i.e., constant speed in a straight line) train, and a second cartoon character (perhaps of the other gender) with a silly name on the station platform.
The person on the train drops a ball, and the ball seems to him as if he is standing still. The person on the platform sees the ball bouncing along with the train guy. The idea is that the train guy cannot tell he is moving, because to him the ball bounces as if he is standing still.
Then a flashlight is brought into the picture, and it turns out that light from the flashlight does not behave this same way, it is not affected by the movement of the train. Apparently, this came as a big surprise. But it should not have come as a surprise, since light is a massless particle/wave, and there is no way that we could have any a priori guess of how it would behave, based on our everyday experience like we have for the ball.
In fact, if you think about it, the ball is a macroscopic object with mass that is being carried along by rigid attachment to the guy’s hand, who in turn is rigidly attached to the floor of the train.
These rigid attachments are not really rigid, but they appear so on the macroscopic level.
Through this series of rigid linkages, and because it has mass, by the most basic and simple rules of inertia, the ball is carried along with the motion of the train. But the light is a different matter. It has no mass, so we should not expect it to be carried along by inertia. The ‘linkage’ to the light beam may very well not be rigid, so even if it did have mass, we would not be confident that the motion of the train would be imparted to the light. I would conclude that either we don’t really know what to expect, or if we
expected something it would be that the light would be uncoupled from the motion of the train.
In fact, light is self-powered, there is not any material object that powers it or pushes it along. As such, in contrast to a ball, we would not expect it to be carried along or affected by the motion of the train. Light travels at the speed of light, if it did not, it would not be light. It cannot stand still. There is nothing you can do to get it started, or to prevent it from starting.
After much fuss and many false starts, it was finally decided that the speed of light is constant, independent of the frame of reference ( e.g., did not matter if you are on the train or on the station platform). That was mysterious (although we can see that it was pretty much expected based on common sense), but it was accepted as TRUTH , and taken as one of several axioms on which special relativity was mathematically derived.
But later, it was shown, by a very accessible mathematical demonstration, that this did not need to be an arbitrary axiom, but it could be mathematically shown that there has to be a speed that is constant (although we did not know that this speed could be identified as being the speed of light). Light Clock
Another aspect of the typical YouTube discussions is a set-up where two mirrors are facing each other, with a small separation. Then one photon light particle is reflected back and forth between the two mirrors. And for good measure, they are set up on our moving train. The geometry of this arrangement is used in a simple derivation of the “Lorenz Contraction,’ a fundamental aspect of the Special Theory of Relativity. The problem is
that light is simultaneously a particle and a wave, and the mirror paradigm ignores that. Also, when something bounces off something else, there is a time delay for the changes of direction (depending on the degree of elasticity of the collision), but this is not included in the paradigm. Finally, the famous Nobel laureate Richard Feynman has demonstrated that the reflection of light is very complicated, not simple like a ball bouncing: light is a quantum object that traverses many paths, not just the path you have in mind.
Inertial Frame of Reference
Another troubling aspect of the way special relativity is explained is the concept of an inertial frame of reference. First of all, there is no such thing as ‘ an inertial frame of reference.’ They must come at least in pairs, since an inertial frame of reference is designated as such only relative to another frame of reference.
For instance, we might think of a train going at a constant speed in a straight line along a track. But this is a complete fiction that could not be further from the truth. There is no absolute preferred frame of reference, but sometimes the so-called ‘cosmic microwave background’ (CMB) are taken as such for the sake of discussion. This is not strictly correct to do, but we are grasping at straws here. Even in the CMB frame of reference, the galaxy and solar system etc. are moving in crazy helical patterns, and the motion of the Earth includes its own rotation and orbiting of the sun on top of all of that [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=POOASc- w8bU&ab_channel=Topaz]. So, a train running on an apparently straight track is most certainly not moving at a constant speed in a straight line. But a second train moving in the same crazy way would be said to be an inertial frame of reference relative to the first train.
There is a strange kind of double-talk that occurs in mathematics, and that has spilled over to special relativity. For instance, in mathematics an irrational number is a number that has an unending (infinite) number of decimal places that are non- repeating. Sometimes there are formulas for generating some special irrational numbers, like pi, but mostly they just have to be written out. Of course, they cannot
be written out because they never end. You cannot even generally tell if two irrational numbers are equal, since they could be identical up to a huge number of decimal places, and then differ at the next decimal place. And the most basic thing in all mathematics is to solve for x = something, and that really cannot be done except in your imagination.
There is no way in reality to tell if two frames of reference are relatively inertial, for how can someone check if the second one is moving at a constant speed in a straight line relative to the first one? This is the same problem as telling if two irrational numbers are equal. Relative the CMB, the apparently straight movement (to us) of the train is actually a tortuous helical motion with all sorts of irregular turns. Do you really think that you could set up another train to move in exactly that same way?
Putting aside all of those problems, let us recall the passenger who is on the moving train. He is presumed to be part of that inertial frame of reference. But let us take a moment to think of the parts of that train. Well, the floor and walls etc . are made of some material, say wood or steel, that are joined together. Unless these joints are 100% rigid, you cannot say they are all part of the same inertial frame of reference, as there will be some vibrations and stretching and compressing on a microscopic (if not macroscopic) level, so even the components within a single frame of reference are not really moving at a constant speed in a straight line relative to one another.
Now add a person to this scenario. Is this person supposed to be bolted to the floor, and as rigid as a concrete statue?
What are we to assume about the walls and windows? Do they surround the car of the train so perfectly, and are they air tight? They better be, or again we have a failure at being an inertial frame of reference.
What about the air within the car of the train? If there is a living person inside the car, then presumably the person breathes air. But the air is compressible, it is not a rigid body. And if the walls and windows are not
100% airtight, the air inside the car will escape and intermix with the outside air. This does not make for a very good inertial frame of reference.
Finally, we know that solid objects are composed of molecules, and molecules do not actually touch. There might be some force trying to merge two objects, and the molecules might get compressed closer and closer to one another, but they never touch, and this microscopic compression would also prevent this from being an inertial frame of reference.
Empirical Evidence
One of the big breakthroughs in physics was the realization that physical laws must be based on empirical evidence, not based on a ‘thought’ experiment. But the concept of inertial frames of reference are not in the realm of empirical reality, they are in the realm of someone’s imagination. And inertial frames of reference are the basis of special relativity.
