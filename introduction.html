INTRODUCTION
I consider topics relating to human knowledge and belief, and the external reality on which these are presumably based. Perhaps the most important statement in this regard is that any argument along these lines must be begging the question. For how can we put forth such an analysis without assuming that there is some sort of external reality and that we can know something (however flawed or inaccurate) about it?
To be able to make even a tiny start in this, it is essential to recognize that there are those things that really are ( i.e., physical objects), and there are those abstractions that are purely the product of the human mind. With respect to the latter, we are not so much concerned here with physical objects that have been manufactured by humans, or produced (even uniquely) by humans as a result of, say, metabolism. The focus is rather on the human-generated thoughts, abstractions, interpretations, overlays, inventions, value judgements, morals, and so forth. To make any progress, we must be able to recognize/distinguish whether a particular thing might have existed even if humans never came into being, or if that thing could only have existed as a consequence of the existence of human brains.
Just as I have mentioned my concerns about begging the question, I am painfully aware that any attempt to motivate someone to read this manuscript must appeal to human brain-related interpretations rather than to the more fundamental real physical objects. Even worse, the fact that I consider real physical objects to be more fundamental in some sense, is itself a human brain- generated value judgement. We are so awash in human brain-
generated overlays that it is next to impossible for us to even recognize them as such, let alone to try to function in some other way. And any argument that it would be better to try to do so would in itself be an example of a human brain-generated value judgement.
Ultimately, any motivation in itself seems to be the product of the human brain. We may as well base our motivation on a clearly- stated preference, rather than try to disguise it as being more fundamental than it really is. My motivation is the expectation/hope that as humans, we would like to see an improved version of the human condition come about and persist. My hypothesis is that this improvement is more likely to be achieved by functioning in reality rather than by functioning in bias. However, I am not certain of this, as it could be that reality is very bleak indeed, and humans may need a distraction from this bleak reality in order to have sufficient motivation to do those things that are necessary for survival.
The most compelling motivation for this undertaking is to try to understand and address the core problem of our existence rather than the peripheral symptoms. Maybe this is too hard to do, because of two reinforcing reasons:
• The symptoms do appear to us to be of paramount importance for our immediate existence and well-being.
• It does in fact take a substantial investment to try to address the core issues.
Maybe the core is too hard for us to deal with, and we feel that we need to do something , so as a substitute we deal with the symptoms. Of course, the problem with that is that we just keep going around in circles, since solving a symptom still leaves the underlying core problem festering. If someone has cancer and a hangnail, it will do no good to trim the hangnail without treating the cancer.
