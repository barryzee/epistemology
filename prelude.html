PRELUDE
AN INITIAL ATTEMPT AT
UNDERSTANDING OURSELVES
At this time, we are uniquely positioned to make an initial attempt at understanding ourselves and how we relate to external reality.
We have a knowledge base derived from the broad fields of physics, cosmology, evolution, genomics, neurobiology, and sociobiological, among others.
We have specific knowledge of the age of earth and the universe, the pathways of nucleosynthesis, the theory of relativity, biological evolution, and functional brain imaging.
We are the beneficiaries of an increasingly enhanced ability to communicate and transmit information, and of a widely- distributed education infrastructure.
In addition to scientific and technological knowledge, we have an impressive body of historical knowledge. In particular, there are a large number of analyses of human behavior during the Medieval period. These studies are an object lesson in the misery of mass ignorance, superstition, and hardship.
Those prevailing conditions were exploited by demagogues and shameless tyrants in religious institutions. They mercilessly sought to achieve their own ends and to leverage their own power, by preying (pun intended) upon the desperation and fears of the masses.
Much of religious dogma today is little more than a re- manifestation (or perhaps continuation) of the mythology disseminated by those Medieval demagogues and tyrants. If we cannot learn today from these abominations of the past, then we are doomed to fall victim to modern day counterparts of the Medieval demagogues and tyrants.
This is happening throughout much of the world, and is particularly exemplified by the stranglehold that the tyrant and demagogue Donald Trump exerts in the United States, largely predicated upon his “base” (pun intended) of ignorant zealots, and his cadre of shameful and shameless henchmen.
If we can free ourselves of our biases (or at least try to reduce the impact of them upon our thinking), the combined power of our resources should be able to help us get a better handle on underlying reality and our place in it.
The challenge now is not lack of knowledge or lack of resources, but rather our own difficulty in being sufficiently motivated and sufficiently courageous to shed crippling biases and prejudices.
