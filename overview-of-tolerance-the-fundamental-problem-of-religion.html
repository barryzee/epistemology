OVERVIEW OF TOLERANCE - THE FUNDAMENTAL PROBLEM OF RELIGION
Tolerance is a funny thing. If you really agree with something, you don’t need to be tolerant, since you already agree with it and respect it. You need tolerance only for those things that you disagree with. Would you want to be married to someone who merely tolerated you? Would you want to married to someone who you merely tolerated? No one would choose to be in a relationship that is based on tolerance.
What about religious tolerance? Well, I suppose that tolerance is better than intolerance, but perhaps we can do a little better than that. Consider the nature of human religious thought. It is best to start with an overall inventory, to make sure we are not being tricked by focusing our attention on one particular version that happens to be familiar or comfortable to us. There has been a vast number of different religions [1]. This vastness strongly suggests that there is no one true answer about the underlying reality, but rather a multiplicity of human ideas and beliefs. This brings us to Table 2 in the chapter EPISTOMOLGY TABLES. In the column entitled “DOMINANT MODES” there is the entry “ NON-RATIONAL” which is designated as “ CONFUSION OF SUBJECTIVE BELIEF/FEELING WITH KNOWLEDGE OF OBJECTIVE REALITY.” Herein lies a major problem, especially vis a vis the concept of tolerance: trying to make someone else believe what you do. See section “ GUIDELINES FOR PERSONAL SUBJECTIVE RELIGIOUS BELIEF ” for a discussion of this problem area.
