THE LIMBIC SYSTEM
The limbic system, which primarily embodies emotions, evolved 150 million years before the cerebral cortex, which primarily embodies rational thought .
Motivation and Evolution
My project comprises more than a dozen chapters, but this single italicized sentence is the crux of the entire matter. This is the material manifestation of the point at which hundreds of thousands of years of evolution interface with contemporary human culture, society, and civilization (Table 1). This is the cause and effect. This is where the tire hits the road.
Tug of War between Rational and Non-Rational
150 million years ago, rational thought was not essential for biological survival. But given the density, complexity, and propensity of weapons of mass destruction of contemporary human society, rational thought (perhaps tempered with a respect for humanity) is now essential. Nuclear annihilation is certainly a concrete and grave threat, but it is only a symptomatic manifestation of the underlying core threat to humanity: the predominance of the limbic over the rational . Given how ancient and deep-seated our limbic system is, we now face an extraordinary challenge, as well as an extraordinary opportunity: to get our limbic systems under control .

Historical and Philosophical Aspects
The histories of human thought and philosophy follow a similar path regardless of the specific topic, be it emotion, religion, the nature of space and time, planetary motion, etc . There are certain phenomena that have long captured our imagination, and still continue to do so.
Our desire to understand ourselves has often led to mystical or religious explanations. In general, the interpretations were wildly inaccurate, and often served to put humans and our planet in an exalted self-aggrandizing position.
Humans used either partial information or overlays and inventions to (mis)interpret the phenomena. Inevitably and unfortunately, those overlays and inventions became cultural memes that still pervade our way of looking at things.
Considering the fleetingly brief duration of human existence, it is really in the last “nanosecond” that we have developed the science and technology to begin to rationally address the underlying causes of those interesting phenomena. The overlays and inventions and cultural memes became so embedded that even when a more rational interpretation becomes available, people remain invested in the standardized mis-interpretation.
There are several reasons that people tend to cling to non-rational explanations (Table 3). For instance, perhaps only a small portion of the human population had been educated (during their childhood) to be able to seek out correct information and to

Brain as a Mechanical Linkage or as an Instrument of Free Will?
Each aspect of human behavior and emotion is controlled by some network of brain regions. Just above, we had very briefly
13
reviewed reward and addiction controlled by one such network, namely the limbic system.
This control can be viewed alternatively as deterministic or perhaps partially subject to a degree of uncertainty that is associated with thermodynamic and quantum noise [9].
The earliest single-cell organisms did not have what we would now consider to be brains. Instead they had a mechanical coupling between:
• specialized protein molecules that act as chemo-sensors
• and flagellar motors that move the organism in a direction according to what was sensed by the chemo-sensors ( i.e. , towards food or away from toxin)
According to [10]
For bacteria that swim, determining whether to stay the course or head in a new direction is vital to survival. A new study offers atomic-level details of the molecular machinery that allows swimming bacteria to sense their environment and change direction when needed.
. . .
“On its surface, a bacterium has thousands of receptors that scan the environment and then tell it what to do,” he said. This is very much like the sensory input that all animals must process. Of course, bacteria are single-celled organisms and don’t have brains, he said. But they nonetheless manage to organize and “remember” sensory signals long enough to respond to them in a way that aids their own survival.
The receptors on the surface of a bacterial cell detect light, chemicals, edible things and poisonous things, and transmit that information to a
14
deeper layer of proteins, called kinases, which interpret this data and translate it into a simple choice: “Keep going” or “Change direction!”
“To know how this mechanical system works, we need to know the structure,” he said.
. . .
The gears of the bacterial brain are now in place.”
These mechanisms were in place remarkably early in the history of life on Earth, as well as very early in the history of Earth itself. The age of the Earth is estimated to be 4.54 billion years [11]. Divergence time estimates for the major groups of eubacteria are between 2.5–3.2 billion years ago while those for archaebacteria are mostly between 3.1–4.1 billion years ago [12]. These mechanisms were already in place in the eubacteria and archaebacteria [13].
The decision of responding to food versus toxin make these couplings seem a little more brain-like, but the outcome was still just part of a mechanical coupling, not what we would think of as a free-will based decision.
As evolution proceeded, these mechanical couplings became implemented through distributed nervous systems and then identifiable entities that we would recognize as brains [14]. This progression would reflect an enhancement of decision-making and organization, but there was no step at which we could say that free will was introduced into the system.
The progress of knowledge creates a dilemma for us. Several decades ago, before the advent of functional brain imaging, we might have been more able to entertain the illusion that love is a

special and authentic emotion. Now that there is a demonstrable physical mechanism, we need to ask whether we are just being fooled by something that might be no more special than a mechanical coupling between a chemoreceptor and flagella.

Once there is a discernible physical mechanism, we can never quite be sure whether that mechanism is serving us or whether we are the servants of that mechanism .
