CULTS
There are certain internal characteristics that make a cult into a cult. One thing that helps to make it recognizable is that it is so different than the rest of society. But what if a particular cult is so pervasive throughout society that there is no recognizable difference, because there is no ‘rest of society’? What if even ‘normal’ people are inadvertently part of the cult, so you do not have concerned parents or friends desperately trying to pull you back from its clutches?
What if the very state of being a human makes you crave the recognition and respect afforded by shared values and beliefs? The problem is that the shared values and beliefs may very well be nothing more than subjective narratives or overlays, and no more true or correct than some other set of values and beliefs. The shared values may themselves comprise a cult, but not recognized as such because of its pervasiveness.
Are there concepts that humans might cling to in spite of them being based on subjective narratives or overlays, in spite of them doing more harm than good, and in spite of the possibility of much better alternatives that are rejected out of hand?
I happen to play a certain online card game from time to time. I noticed three traits of some of the other players, that often co-occur so often and so strongly that it gives the impression of being a ‘syndrome’
• Ignorance of the correct strategy and tactics of the game, which involves incorrectly blaming another player for their own bad score
• Abusive behavior manifested by vulgar language aimed at the other ‘at fault’ player
• Misogyny manifested by an insult that is formed by assuming (or it seems like ‘accusing’) the other ‘at fault’ player is a woman and
intensified by vulgarisms along those lines, that need not be repeated here
As disgusting as these behaviors are per se , they have increased in frequency and in brazenness since the advent of Trump as president. It is clear that his manifestation of these behaviors normalizes them for these other at risk individuals, and gives them the encouragement to practice them more openly. This can be taken as some characteristics of a cult of inappropriate ‘blaming’.
I have in mind two phenomena that might (or might not) be cults in disguise. The disguise comes from the nearly universal occurrence of these potential cults. In particular, I am considering political affiliation and religious affiliation. I am more or less just putting these on the table for consideration and open discussion and debate. Even to me they seem like they may be too extreme, too much of an overstatement. What do you think?

Religious Affiliation
Let us next consider religious affiliation. I have covered many aspects of religion in other sections of this manuscript. Here I want to explore whether religion per se could be thought of as a cult. I am not talking about certain particular examples of widely known small cults, but I am talking about the very act of religion as it is widely praticed throughout the world.
Of course, if one individual happens to hold a unique religious idea that is not shared with anyone else, that is clearly not a cult. It is just an individual exercising a commonly accepted right to have whatever subjective belief one wishes, whether true or false in external objective reality.
If you search google, you will see that there are page after page of named religions. But I am talking about the small number of religions that together comprise the bulk of humanity. To avoid the appearance of hand- waving, Figure 1 shows some hard data from Pew Research [6]. The take- away message for the current discussion is not the specific breakdown of
particular religions, but that 16.3% are unaffiliated, This leaves 83.7% as being affiliated.
Figure 1. Data from Pew Research: Size of major religious groups
The 16.3 % unaffiliated shows that at least in some places people are free to choose. Based on my cursory examination, there are some countries where there is essentially zero freedom, and other places where there is hypothetically some freedom but in practice there is generally strict adherence. In my opinion, the study of cults depends on a more or less free choice to become part of the cult, followed by the strict retention once inside. So this criterion would whittle down the 83.7% figure, as a substantial fraction of these might be more or less coerced. Pulling a figure out of thin air, let us ‘agree’ that 50% of people worldwide voluntarily ‘chose’ to associate with one of the major religions. This is the group that I would like to assess as either being in a cult or not being in a cult.
Here are some criteria that I would use to explore ‘cultness’
• Why not just have the belief on your own rather than feeling the need to be associated so closely with like-minded people on a global scale?
• The beliefs of the group are not based on fact, but rather are based more on myth or narratives or overlays
• More harm than good comes of those beliefs
Why not just have the belief on your own rather than feeling the need to be associated so closely with like-minded people on a global scale?
No one knows whether or not god exists in objective external reality, in spite of how strongly you might hold a subjective personal belief. Given the diversity of individuals’ beliefs and the fact that most of the belief systems are mutually exclusive, it is likely that either a vast number of
people have beliefs that are not correct in objective external reality. It might even be the case that god does not exist in objective external reality, and therefore every belief system is incorrect.
No matter how confident someone is about their belief, deep down there is probably some smidgeon of doubt. Perhaps the reinforcement of and mutual respect and undersanding of other people can help allay the lingering doubt? This might be a reason to seek religious associations.
Another reason might be to seek political power to leverage public or governmental blessing for your belief. It is well known that Trump leveraged conservative religious groups and fundamentalist groups as voting blocs.
In my opinion, neither of these is a ‘good’ reason to form the associations.
The beliefs of the group are not based on fact, but rather are based more on myth or narratives or overlays
Of course, one of the most basic rights in America is freedom of religion. Each person can hold any religious belief that they choose. If there were to exist absolute proof of the correctness, in objective reality, of a particular religious belief, then presumably there would not be room for a diverity of views. For instance, we do not have some portion of the population that insists that two plus two equals the square toot of seventy nine. That is because there is absolute proof, in objective reality, that two plus two equals four. Religious belief is a matter of faith, not a matter of fact. As such, it is based not on fact but on myth, narrative, and overlays.
Much of the good that is attributed to religion is actually in the province of human decency, not in the province of religion
For example, feeding the hungry might be carried out in a church by people who go to that church, but this is basically human decency, it is not something that god needs to be involved in. Even in a world that had no churches and no concept of god or religion, there would still be well- intentioned people or perhaps government agencies feeding the hungrry and sheltering the homeless.
I am not going to make an exhaustive list of such activities, but if you think about it you will see that a lot of positive stuff related to a church is not basically religious in nature.
More harm than good comes of those beliefs
The amount of harm that came from religion is enormous. Persecution of Jewish peoples, simply because they are Jewish, is a scourge of human kind that has spanned millenia, and culminated with the murder of some six million souls by the Nazis. The backlash to this horror is itself a horror. In a misguided effort to secure Israel, Benjamin Netenyahu directed the sickening murder of some seventy thousand people in Gaza.
What is really distressing is that no one knows if god exists in objective reality, yet vast numbers of people are willing to subscribe to what must amount to overlain narratives and invented myths, none of which is likely to bear any relation to the underlying reality. Yet person A will persecute
or even murder person B, simply because they each subscribe to a different invented mythology, both of which are incorrect!
