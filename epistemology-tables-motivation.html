EPISTEMOLOGY TABLES MOTIVATION
There are two tables that represent the core of my manuscript. The contents of the two tables are available in a separate chapter called “EPISTEMOLOGY TABLES.” The preliminary current remarks can best be read before looking at the tables per se. The associated comments below are intended to make this core more understandable by providing additional context.
The two tables are my attempt to capture the nature of external reality and the nature of the human brain, and how they inter- relate.
The human brain is an object that came about as the result of biological evolution. It exists. As such, it is primarily an object that has the rather unusual property of survivability, relative to the reality in which it exists. It is impossible to understand the human brain, and therefore, who we are, without understanding that it is a surviving product of biological evolution.
So we have one half of our equation
external reality → human brain
On the other hand, our knowledge of external reality is based upon the sensory apparatus of the human brain. We take our perceptions, and our brains’ interpretations of the perceptions, as being much more accurate than they really are (see the chapter “INVENTION”). A lot of our problems would be diminished if we could pull back from this “arrogance,” and not be so sure of the correctness of our beliefs.
So we have the other half of our equation
human brain → external reality
Our two equations can now be combined in the form of a self- referential cycle
external reality
human brain
It might be helpful to review this self-referential cycle in the context of the foregoing discussion and in the context of other chapters in this manuscript:
• Evolution, in the context of the prevailing external reality, resulted in the object that we call the human brain. That process of evolution endowed the human brain with a certain degree of survivability, otherwise it would no longer be in existence. That survivability is composed of two main parts:
(1) reasonably accurate perception of e.g. a lion that might attack at any moment, and
evolves from
interprets
(2) emotional interpretations that are largely governed by overlays. These overlays act to make life seem more palatable than it might actually be.
• The other side of the coin is that we then use and rely on this brain to obtain our perception of external reality. But those perceptions are distorted by some of those very same properties that make the human brain survivable, namely emotional interpretations that are largely governed by overlays.
COMMENTS ABOUT TABLE 1
The contents of the two tables are available in a separate chapter called “EPISTEMOLOGY TABLES.” This would be a good time to view that chapter while reading the remainder of this current chapter.
Table 1 focuses primarily on external reality, although it does contain one bonus column about human belief. Table 1 is comprehensive as well as compact. It is remarkable that so huge a topic can be captured in so simple a table.
There are 4 categories, each of which can be true or false (these truth values are given in columns 2-5), giving a maximum of 2^4=16 possibilities. However, 4 of these possibilities, in which column 2 is false and column 3 is true, are not feasible, so only 12 possibilities are listed in the table. Of these 12, only lines 1 and 2 are associated with a valid belief, namely those in which the truth is currently knowable and human belief aligns with actual reality. The remaining 10 lines are associated with invalid beliefs.
The reason for lines 3 and 4 being invalid is simply that human belief is contradicted by reality. For example, someone might believe that the earth is flat.
A good example for lines 5 through 8 would be to go back in time to say 1950, and to make a statement about the composition of moon rocks. Since this would have been unknowable at that time, such statements would be invalid (regardless of whether they randomly happened to be true or false). These are not examples
of lines 9 through 12, even in 1950, as they are potentially knowable in the future.
An example of lines 9 through 12 might be something like the question of the existence of god, as that might never be knowable, regardless of the sophistication of future technology.
It is left as an exercise for the advanced reader to augment Table 1 by adding another “truth value” to column 5, namely “D” for “Don’t know.”
COMMENTS ABOUT TABLE 2
Table 2 focuses primarily on modes of human thought about external reality, rather than about the external reality itself. In a sense, this is a more detailed view of the contents of column 5 in Table 1, i.e., how did the T or F (or the augmented version with D’s) in that column come about?
There are 2 dominant modes, rational and non-rational. I prefer “non-rational” rather than “irrational,” as the latter carries with it a sense of negative value judgement, which I do not mean to imply. Non-rational is associated with an individual’s confusion of subjective belief/feeling with knowledge of objective reality.
A word about objective reality, which I suggest/hope can be ascertained to some degree by the rational mode. This mode is presumably our best hope of achieving lines 1 or 2 of Table 1 (or another VALID line in the augmented version of Table 1). What this comes down to, is that the human brain trying its best to make some sort of empirical observation in an un-premeditated, unbiased, objective manner; and the more cerebral parts of the brain (in contrast to, say, the limbic system – see the chapter “INVENTION”) are making some sort of inference or interpretation.
Anecdotally, I am tempted to quote Sherlock Holmes:
"It is a capital mistake to theorize before one has data. Insensibly one begins to twist facts to suit theories, instead of theories to suit facts."
A Scandal in Bohemia , Sir Arthur Conan Doyle.
Ironically, Sherlock may have elsewhere been guilty of such theorizing:
“It was invisible, buried in the mud. I only saw it because I was looking for it.”
“What! You expected to find it?”
“I thought it not unlikely.”
Silver Blaze , Sir Arthur Conan Doyle.
We might think of testing the validity of this whole process by asking whether a large number of trained people independently agree on the observation and interpretation. But many objections to this criterion immediately leap out at us, primarily that trained people are not really independent entities.
For instance, the physical and biological makeup of the human brain are basically similar from person to person, so in a sense we are just looking at the functioning of a large number of “clones,” and it is not surprising that these can behave similarly, even when the observation and interpretation are not necessarily in line with external truth.
Trained people may be subject to biases in schools of thought that are in vogue across the institutions that create trained people.
And I am sure there are several more objections. I believe that both the observation and the interpretation are subject to these
limitations, but perhaps some forms of observation might be more reliable, such as instrumental readings.

Allport [2] has described the personality type characterized by very low tolerance of ambiguity (I will call this the “LTA personality type”). Whereas a more or less healthy individual might be willing/able to accept that the first two columns of Table 1 can very well contain values of F, the LTA type will place a huge premium on being able to make a definite statement rather than on making a valid statement. This can lead to over-
dependence and overly literal interpretations of dogma or documents that had been created by all too imperfect humans.
The comments I made above regarding the clonal behavior of the human brain is particularly apropos here. In fact, there is a danger that the large number of adherents, that might result from the clonal behavior, could mistakenly be taken as evidence of validity. And even if not taken as evidence of validity, the large number of adherents could make it uncomfortable/dangerous for others in that society, so there might be a pressure to adhere even without an actual acceptance of the dogma.
I have in mind two particular classes of documents (1) constitutions and (2) bibles. These are just the kinds of things that the LTA type will point to as reflecting absolute reality.
The US Constitution was written primarily by slave owners [3], it discriminated against everyone except propertied white males, and provided for the legalization of massive slavery. Ten of the first twelve presidents were major slave owners [4]. I certainly do not think that we want to consider documents written by such men, i.e., the US Constitution, as reflecting the basis of what our principles should be.
These considerations cast a shadow over the validity/authority of the US Supreme Court. I always find it amusing to listen to recordings of Supreme Court proceedings. The tortuous arguments relating to precedents always seem silly. It would be more reasonable to apply a measure of common sense and human decency to solve a problem, rather than treating the body of laws
relating to the US Constitution as if they reflect a mathematical system.
Unlike a set of mathematical axioms and theorems, human laws are severely flawed. An attempt at deductive reasoning based on a set of flawed laws will lead to a flawed result. Not to mention that the composition of the Supreme Court is a collection of political appointees who are usually selected to reflect the political views of the current president. If the composition of the Court were different, the decisions might very well be the reverse. This is not the way a mathematical system would work ( i.e., deduction does not depend on the particular mathematician), and the decisions of the Court should not be treated with the degree of reverence that they currently enjoy.
Bibles, similarly, were written by men, and reflect the values of those writers. Bibles are most reasonably taken as a mixture of (1) “based on a true story” with appropriate dramatic embellishments, and (2) total embellishments. The contents of such a document is best taken as a metaphor of desirable behavior, influenced by the prevailing beliefs and attitudes of the time.
To whatever limited extent the “authentic” version of the bible (discounted for the inaccuracies and biases inherent in human translation from the original Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek) are to be relied upon, we also need to deal with the weighty edifice of church hierarchy that is built upon the frail “authentic” underpinnings. This will undoubtedly be rife with self-interest and self-serving motives and all of the other toxins that are inherent in just about any human organization/institution of any size. After all, how many peoples’ livelihoods and incomes and
self-importance stem from their positions in the church, and depend on the continued existence of the church? This aspect is summarized in a subsequent chapter “TIME-LINE OF THE ORIGIN AND CORRUPTION OF RELIGIOUS THOUGHT.”
This is the appropriate place to emphasize the “wishful- thinking/serves me” phenomenon. This can underlie one of the most insidious problems, namely the blurring of the line between wishful thinking and reality. Also, the semantic problem of positing “that is my reality.” “Reality” should refer to a unique version of external reality, not to a personalized variant that is in one person’s realm of wishful thinking. No that there is anything wrong with some wishful thinking, just that wishful thinking should be called “wishful thinking” and not be confused with “external reality.”
No matter how strongly you may believe something, that does not make it a reality, it just makes it a belief that you strongly hold. This might be most difficult in the realm of religion, where a combination of circumstances may conspire to trick you: (1) beliefs may be comforting in contrast to a reality that seems devastating, (2) you are immersed in a group of people (or an entire culture, see my comments about the clonal nature of human thought) who all think the way you do and therefore seems to reinforce the validity of something that is in fact not valid, (3) your parents, school, etc . have reinforced this view every moment of your life since the day you were born, (4) politicians and other demagogues reinforce this way of thinking in order to control you and in order to get your vote, (5) your education or profession or culture was such as to not train you to think critically.
The related phenomenon of “wishful-thinking/serves me” arises when the individual is somewhat aware of the distinction between the concepts of their belief and external reality, but they take great comfort in their beliefs and accept a pragmatic approach to comforting themselves in their lives, rather than a relentless search for what is true. Again, this might involve some sorts of religious beliefs. If one were to embark on the slippery slope of making value judgements, I would say that, in contrast to the person of the previous paragraph who confuses their personal belief with external reality, at least this person has an awareness of the dichotomy between their beliefs and the (perhaps unknowable) external reality, and they do not confuse the two.
Quite apart from the literal content of constitutions (except perhaps constitutions for countries whose names start with “The Peoples’ Democratic Republic of . . .”) and bibles, we might hope that they abstractly, as opposed to literally, reflect the “best hopes of humankind.”
