OVERVIEW OF THE NATURE OF TIME
According to relativity, the perception of a “now”, and particularly of a “now” that moves along in time so that time appears to “flow”, therefore arises purely as a result of human consciousness and the way our brains are wired, perhaps as an evolutionary tool to help us deal with the world around us, even if it does not actually reflect the reality. As Einstein himself remarked, “People like us, who believe in physics, know that the distinction between past, present, and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion” [1].
Motivation
Our ability to understand the nature of time is essential for the study of epistemology. If past and future are inherently different, then there is at least a hope that there is such a thing as cause and effect, and, therefore, a hope that there is such a thing as free will.
On the other hand, say that there is no difference between past and future. Since we know that the past has already happened, then that means that the future has already happened. Then we must call into question whether there is such a thing as such thing as temporal ordering of events - whether it has any meaning to say that event A caused event B. If everything that we do has no consequence, what does that say about the meaning of free will?
Past, Present, and Future
A persistent problem in philosophy and physics is the comparative nature of past time and future time. Think of “present time” or “now” as an infinitely thin razor that can cut time into
“past time” and “future time,” in the sense of a “Dedekind Cut” [2]; perhaps “now” does not have a finite existence.
So, another unresolved controversy might be whether “now” really exists in external reality, or if “now” is simply a human concept, perhaps of a psychological nature. Something like a “point” in geometry, which obviously cannot exist in reality (real objects are made up of particles, which, however small, have finite non-zero size). It is commonly believed that the past is, by definition, determined. In contrast, it is commonly believed that the future is yet to be determined.
Here is an excellent and readable on-line tutorial [1] on the meaning of time within the Theory of Relativity. I highly recommend reviewing this tutorial in order to be better able to understand my discussions about time in the next few sections.
With the advent of the Theory of Relativity [1], [3], there may be a theoretical basis for assuming that, contrary to the common or popular view, the future is, in fact, partially or fully, determined. That is, the physical nature of the future is no different than the physical nature of the past.
I have sometimes glanced at a few books and articles about whether the future already exists. My overall impression is that it is an open question, with people, who are much more knowledgeable and gifted than myself, on both sides of the issue. It would seem that, at the present time (no pun intended) we can do no better than to treat this as a philosophical question. Presumably, there is a physical reality, albeit not determinable at
the moment (and perhaps never) underlying the philosophical speculation.
