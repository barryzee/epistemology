SUMMARY
This manuscript explores some aspects of epistemology [1].
Epistemology (from
Greek
ἐπιστήμη
, epistēmē , meaning 'knowledge', and
-logy
) is the branch of
philosophy
concerned with the theory of
knowledge
.
Epistemology is the study of the nature of knowledge, justification, and the rationality of belief. Much debate in epistemology centers on four areas:
(1) the
philosophical analysis
of the nature of knowledge and how it relates to such concepts as
truth
,
belief
, and
justification
(2) various problems of
skepticism
(3) the sources and scope of knowledge and justified belief
(4) the criteria for knowledge and justification. Epistemology addresses such questions as: "What makes justified beliefs justified?", "What does it mean to say that we know something?", and fundamentally "How do we know that we know?"
I consider topics relating to human knowledge and belief, and the external reality on which these are presumably based. At this
2
time, we are uniquely positioned to make an initial attempt at understanding ourselves and how we relate to external reality. We have a knowledge base derived from the broad fields of physics, cosmology, evolution, genomics, neurobiology, and sociobiological, among others.
There are two tables (presented in sections entitled ‘EPISTOMOLOGY TABLES’ and ‘EPISTOMOLOGY TABLES MOTIVATION’) that represent the core of my manuscript. The two tables are my attempt to capture the nature of external reality and the nature of the human brain, and how they inter-relate.
The concept of overlays is central to this study, in particular the type of overlay that I refer to as invention . An invention is a story that the human brain creates. It may be that the invention corresponds closely to some aspect of external reality, or it may bear no relationship whatsoever to external reality. Often, it is soon forgotten that the invention was invented, and the invention can be mistakenly considered to be a fundamental truth, that is, it is taken to be an axiom .
In addition to the concepts of overlays and invention, we consider some aspects of the evolution and the functioning of the human brain, the nature of time, and religious and political literalism.
Above all else, the brain is an object that is the product of biological evolution. As such, it senses and interprets external reality sufficiently accurately that it has been able to physically survive.
3
However, that degree of accuracy is perhaps the bare minimum that meets the test of physical survivability. The over-riding characteristic of the brain is that it tricks us. This deception is apparently necessary for psychological survival. A bacterium can survive if endowed with physical survivability. Humans also need that, but they additionally require psychological survivability.
The brain tricks us at every level of its functioning: molecular, neuronal communication, overall systemic integration, emotions, inappropriate categorization and generalization, and false/inappropriate/destructive beliefs.
For example:
• The illusion of temporally continuous visual perception, as well as the illusion of spatially continuous visual perception.
• The functioning of the limbic system.
• The propensity of the brain for overlays and invention.
These examples are discussed in some detail throughout this manuscript.
The brain’s deceptions have been well documented by the authors and domain experts in each of the individual topic areas. However, there are two things that are generally missing:
(1) First, the authors are very careful (and properly so) not to extrapolate their conclusions beyond the restricted
4
boundaries of their areas of expertise. So the way the visual system works is well-documented per se , but the implications of those conclusions are usually confined to the narrow focus of the subject matter. Those implications are not extended to explicit consideration of what they mean vis a vis “who we are.”
(2) Second, there is a lack of unifying integration of the conclusions of each topic. That is, there is not an explicit connection made to inter-relate the conclusions of the individual topics. The overall purpose of my manuscript has been to make these extrapolations and integrations more explicit.
